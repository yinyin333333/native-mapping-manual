Build a pool across all five Acts

Choose among 25 map groups and roll 1–10 traits from 83 player and monster effects.

1. Prepare data2. Configure3. Use the map4. Adapt it

What to create or confirm

This large example preserves a complete 25-group, 83-trait configuration. All groups use existing areas across Acts I–V; no new Levels or monster pools are defined. Check that the intended areas and entrances are usable in your mod.

These examples use one Identify Scroll (isc). Create an exact isc → Red Portal CubeMain recipe: follow the plain-scroll recipe instructions, replacing its tsc input with isc. The TOML route alone does not create this recipe. Confirm IDs against your effective mod data using the ID lookup guide.

The decisions in this configuration

One tier, 25 possible destinations

Each group has weight = 100, so each has a 1-in-25 selection chance (4%). The number of floors in a group does not increase its chance. One craft selects one whole group; its entrances value chooses the landing Level.

Separate fixed rules from random effects

SettingThis example
Every mapMonster Maximum Life +50% from tier base_stats.
Density100–1000% in steps of 1, rolled for the selected group.
Trait count1–10 selected traits. The fixed life bonus and density do not consume trait slots.
CandidatesThe same 83 traits in every group. Each trait has weight 100; player and monster traits share this pool.
Trait valuesEach selected trait uses its own fixed value or range. All Skills rolls +1 or +2; maximum mana rolls +10–30%; mana regeneration rolls +25–75%.

A mixed pool does not guarantee a player bonus or a monster penalty on every map. All Skills, maximum mana and mana regeneration target players. The monster choices include damage, resistance, life, elemental damage and other combat effects.

What to check in the map

The destination comes from one of the 25 groups. The HUD shows tier1, the rolled density, the fixed monster life bonus and the selected trait descriptions. If monster_maximum_life is selected, its +10–30% adds to the fixed +50%, giving a +60–80% map contribution to that stat.

Multi-floor groups share the same result. Re-entering their Levels does not roll new traits. Another craft can select a previously used group again.

Some monster stats depend on the monster’s attacks or skills. A listed trait and its HUD line do not establish that every monster can use that effect. Start with a smaller pool when comparing gameplay.

Use the recipe, enter its portal, and open the automap. Before a later craft replaces a map, collect needed items and corpses and move all players and allies out of every affected Level. See the repeat-craft procedure. Configuration parsing and map rolls are checked separately from gameplay; confirm the effects in your own mod.

Change the behavior intentionally

  • Fewer destinations: remove unwanted group tables while retaining at least one group.
  • A more frequent destination: change one group weight from 100 to 200. Its chance becomes 200 / 2600 (about 7.69%); each other group becomes 100 / 2600.
  • A narrower density range: change each group’s range to { min = 100, max = 200, step = 25 } for 100, 125, 150, 175 or 200%.
  • Fewer random effects: change tier trait_count to { min = 2, max = 4 }. Keep enough candidates in every group.
  • Remove an effect: remove its ID from every relevant trait_pool. Delete the trait definition only after removing its references.

For distinct regional pools, use Different traits by region. To isolate one player stat, use Check All Skills on its own.
